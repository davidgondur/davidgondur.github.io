Bio

Hi! I’m David, a graduate in Computer Engineering and Business Administration from the University of Granada, currently studying for a Master’s Degree in Artificial Intelligence, Pattern Recognition and Digital Imaging at the Universitat Politècnica de València. I’m specialising in Data Science and Computer Vision. I have hands-on experience across the data lifecycle from my internship as a Junior Data Engineer at Cívica Software. I’m interested in Trustworthy AI, particularly uncertainty quantification and interpretability, fields I keep studying to help make intelligent systems transparent, robust and ethical.

I’m currently looking for an internship in which to carry out my master’s thesis! 😉

Note My full story Click to read it

During my university studies I discovered my passion for AI, specifically Machine Learning, which made it possible to build algorithms that went far beyond code and to extract complex patterns from raw data, turning them into deep knowledge. This passion led me naturally to Data Science. I enjoy digging into datasets to uncover hidden patterns and creating visualisations that help explain the reality behind them.

My technical foundation in the data lifecycle was strengthened during my extracurricular internship at Cívica Software, where I worked for 3 months as a Junior Data Engineer on a project for a financial institution. There I gained essential experience in the database ecosystem, which helped me understand the data lifecycle and ETL processes.

Later, thanks to one of the most demanding but rewarding courses of my degree, I developed a strong interest in Computer Vision. This field allowed me to explore classical image representation and filtering, hand-crafted feature extraction and, ultimately, to dive into the fascinating world of Deep Learning, exploring the potential of CNNs and Transformers in complex tasks such as object detection and segmentation.

However, I also realised that AI is not infallible: it not only makes objective mistakes, but also reproduces inaccuracies and systemic biases inherited from human-collected datasets. As a result, I have moved increasingly towards Trustworthy AI. I believe that for artificial intelligence to be truly useful in the real world, it must be transparent and robust.

My most recent focus in this area is Uncertainty Quantification, the core of my Bachelor’s Thesis: Uncertainty quantification in the predictions of machine learning models for biological profile estimation problems (more details below in Projects). This work integrates Conformal Prediction into biological estimation tasks, such as estimating age and sex from maxillofacial X-rays, providing prediction intervals (in regression) and label sets (in classification) with statistical guarantees, thus capturing the uncertainty in each case.

I’m currently reading Interpretable Machine Learning by Christoph Molnar, to get a fuller picture of how to open the “black box” of algorithms.

Beyond academics, I have always believed in taking an active part in the university community. During my degree I had the honour of serving for 1 year as vice-secretary and for 2 years as secretary of the Student Delegation of Computer and Telecommunication Engineering (DEIIT). In this role I was responsible for calling plenary sessions, drafting and publishing the minutes and managing the member register, as well as helping to organise school events (Fine Arts markets, School Day and a video game development Hackathon) or collaborating in others (Granabot).
